## What is happening (with sources)

From late 2023, commercial vessels were targeted in the Red Sea and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, and carriers avoided the Suez Canal by **rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope** at the southern tip of Africa. Per UNCTAD and IMF analysis, container transits through the Suez Canal fell sharply between December 2023 and March 2024, and the Cape detour added roughly **10–14 days each way** on Asia–Europe lanes.

After the October 2025 Gaza ceasefire, attacks in the Red Sea paused, but the diverted routes did not snap back — the Cape of Good Hope routing has remained a de facto standard (per S&P Global and others). The Strait of Hormuz, meanwhile, remains a key chokepoint through which **about 20% each** of global petroleum liquids and LNG transit (EIA, 2024).

## Impact on SMEs (only as far as sources support)

Longer routings feed through as **longer lead times, more volatile freight and insurance costs, and schedule uncertainty** for import-dependent procurement. Smaller firms importing parts in small, frequent lots may need to revisit inventory levels and order timing. Trade-news services such as JETRO's Business Brief let you check the latest item- and region-specific situation.

This article organises facts within what the sources support; it is not a freight or lead-time forecast.

## Practical points to watch

- For key imported items, review procurement lead time and inventory safety margin.
- Treat freight and insurance volatility as an input to contracts and pricing.
- Prepare options in calm periods: alternative suppliers, alternative routings, earlier ordering.
