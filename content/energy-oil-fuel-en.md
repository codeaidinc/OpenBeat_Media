## What is happening (with sources)

The Middle East situation is a standing geopolitical risk for the price and supply of crude oil, LNG and fuels. According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), **about 20% of global petroleum liquids** and **about 20% of global LNG trade** transit the Strait of Hormuz in the Middle East (2024).

The stakes are even higher for Japan: per Japan's Agency for Natural Resources and Energy (ENECHO), **Middle East dependence for crude imports was about 95% in 2024**. The Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) treats the stable supply of fuel and petroleum products under the Middle East situation as an ongoing policy priority.

## Impact on SMEs (only as far as sources support)

Energy-price swings feed through to transport, manufacturing and retail — any business where fuel or energy is a meaningful cost. Cost pass-through can be tracked with the Bank of Japan's **Corporate Goods Price Index (CGPI)**. In the latest reading (March 2026 preliminary), domestic CGPI was +2.6% year on year, and the **yen-based import price index was +7.9%** year on year, signalling import-driven cost pressure.

All figures are as of the cited release. Prices move, so check each source's latest release for current data. This article does not offer a price forecast.

## Practical points to watch

- Know your fuel/energy cost sensitivity (how much a price move changes your bottom line).
- Review pricing and contract terms alongside public indicators such as the CGPI.
- In calm periods, line up the levers you control: efficiency, inventory, procurement timing.
