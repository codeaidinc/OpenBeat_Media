## What is happening (with sources)

When the Middle East situation feeds through to energy prices, the effect shows up — with a lag — in **business-to-business prices**. Japan's **Corporate Goods Price Index (CGPI)**, published by the Bank of Japan, lets you track this. In the March 2026 preliminary reading, domestic CGPI was +2.6% year on year and the **yen-based import price index was +7.9%** year on year, signalling import-driven cost pressure.

Energy is a major component of import prices, and because Japan depends on the Middle East for about 95% of its crude (ENECHO), price moves originating in the Middle East transmit readily into domestic costs via import prices.

## Impact on SMEs (only as far as sources support)

The CGPI is a yardstick for asking: **costs are rising — am I passing them through?** The gap between domestic and import price indices can be read as a rough gauge of pass-through lag and the room to absorb input-cost rises. Figures are as of the cited release; check the Bank of Japan's data list for the latest month.

This article explains how to read public indicators; it does not recommend any specific pricing or outlook.

## Practical points to watch

- Check domestic and import price indices monthly, against your own purchased items.
- Be able to justify the timing and size of price revisions using public indicators such as the CGPI.
- When a weaker yen pushes import prices up, revisit procurement and inventory assumptions.
